# K–2 mystery challenge

Frame a concrete mission with a success condition, a playable 3–7 step sequence, and two or more constraints. Preserve every object, variant, or comparison the parent names in the child actions; never replace a specific request with generic “try a test” language. For comparisons, say what to build or select, what stays the same, what changes, how many trials to run, and what result to mark. Prefer reconfiguring one object between trials when that makes the comparison fairer and faster; require multiple builds only when the learning mechanic truly needs them. Populate `evidence_rows` with the short child-facing name of every compared version so the printable becomes a usable recording grid. Allow multiple valid approaches. Provide a three-step hint ladder from gentle nudge to strong help, but do not reveal the solution immediately. Ask the child to produce evidence—a drawing, test result, arrangement, or explanation—then reflect on what changed. Favor adventure, mystery, construction, comparison, and playful problem solving over quiz language.
